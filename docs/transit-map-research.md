## Seven implementation rules

1. **Use long straight runs and purposeful bends.** TfL **rule:** direction changes are 45° or 90° (§6, p.10). MTR **observation:** extended horizontal/vertical runs with rounded transitions, e.g. Tsuen Wan–Prince Edward. **Inference:** remove short alternating doglegs unless they solve a station, label, or crossing constraint; an angular grid alone does not produce convincing routes.
2. **Design actual radii, including adjacent curves.** TfL **rule:** the innermost adjacent curve uses radius 3x; x is route thickness/station-name x-height (§§5–6, pp.8,10). **Inference:** choose a common radius family relative to stroke width. Offset corridor curves concentrically; do not round independently displaced polylines and expect equal separation.
3. **Keep route colors distinct in corridors.** MTR **observation:** red/green remain side by side between Prince Edward, Mong Kok and Yau Ma Tei; orange/teal also retain separate traces west of Kowloon. These examples show diagrammatic co-running, not proof of shared physical track. **Inference:** preserve lane order and spacing through bends; avoid coincident colored strokes that hide one route.
4. **Encode transfers, not every crossing.** TfL **rule:** shared-track/platform interchange circles sit between lines; platform changes use multiple/bridged circles (§8, p.12). MTR **observation:** transfer capsules at Mong Kok differ from the unmarked green/light-blue crossing west of Ho Man Tin. **Inference:** reserve shared markers for declared common skills. Use a small background casing/gap at an unrelated crossing if needed for clarity; that treatment is our choice, not a cited mandate.
5. **Separate terminal from continuation.** TfL **rule:** double tabs mark line ends; arrows mean continuation beyond the diagram; an interchange terminal retains a ring (§10.1, p.19). MTR **observation:** termini such as Tsuen Wan use ordinary station circles. **Inference:** select one consistent vocabulary; do not combine operator terminal grammars accidentally. A line badge identifies a route, not its stopping point.
6. **Place labels as a system.** MTR **observation:** labels align in rows above Tsuen Wan's horizontal run and beside the eastern vertical run. Tokyo's **stated convention** reinforces names with line/station codes rather than color alone. **Inference:** group labels on a consistent side of each run, reserve clear label bands before routing, and check actual text bounds; retain route letters alongside colors.
7. **Make trains obey the same geometry and semantics.** **Inference from these static diagrams, not an operator animation standard:** move each train by distance along its own final SVG path, orient it to the tangent, and keep it in its colored corridor lane. Use continuous motion through bends, explicit endpoint reversal/dwell, and no implied line switching at unmarked crossings. Do not teleport from one terminus to the other. Verify station stops against the final curve, not the raw control polygon.

## Critique of the supplied source

The reusable pieces already exist: hand-authored `Line.path`, ordered `Line.stations`, independent station/label coordinates, route letters/colors, and `roundedPath`. Keep those data structures unless the renderer reveals a concrete gap. All present control-polygon segments are horizontal, vertical, or 45°; adding another grid restriction would not address the main problem.

- **S is the strongest geometry candidate** (line 88): seven direction changes, several 32-unit or roughly 34-unit legs, then a 488-unit horizontal run. Half-segment clamping leaves some consecutive bends without an intervening straight. Simplify this stepped approach while retaining TypeScript → Next.js → Cloudflare → Here and the reserved title region.
- **P/M hide a corridor decision** (lines 38,58): both start at React `(680,64)` and share the initial vertical control segment. Rounded P departs slightly earlier, but the strokes still coincide initially. Decide whether to show a short parallel departure or an explicitly shared terminal symbol; do not rely on paint order.
- **Crossings need explicit review:** S crosses H at `(920,88)` without shared station membership. S also intersects P's diagonal at `(736,144)`, distinct from Next.js at `(680,144)`. These are geometric intersections, not declared transfers. Their actual visual treatment cannot be established from this file.
- **`radius` is a trim distance** (lines 131–145): the function cuts equal distances from each vertex and emits SVG `Q`, a quadratic Bézier. It is tangent to adjoining segments but is not a circular arc. For a circular fillet with deflection angle θ, cutback is `R × tan(θ/2)`; equal cutbacks therefore do not mean equal radius for 45° and 90° turns. The default 22 also shrinks silently on short legs, creating inconsistent bends.
- **Keep stations off fillets:** Vue `(624,232)` is only two units beyond M's computed straight-section start `(626,232)` when traversing leftward; its marker may intrude into the bend depending on stroke/marker size. Check every final station clearance after routing. Labels already have explicit positions/anchors, so reposition those rather than adding a layout engine.
- **Terminals/animation remain unverified:** station markers are dot/ring/here, without a dedicated terminal variant. That is not inherently wrong given MTR's practice. This source contains no train sampling, timing, orientation, or crossing masks, so it cannot prove animation or rendering defects. Duplicate consecutive path points would cause division by zero in `roundedPath`; current data do not contain them.

## Concrete direction

Keep the hand-laid network. First simplify S and establish visible P/M departure lanes; preserve genuine shared stations and leave unrelated crossings unconnected. Set one stroke-relative bend family and adequate straight approaches around stops, then evaluate whether circular SVG arcs are needed in the existing helper. Reposition the existing labels around those runs. Finally drive train position and heading from exactly the displayed path, with endpoint behavior explicit. A broader routing framework or new dependency is unnecessary for six routes.
